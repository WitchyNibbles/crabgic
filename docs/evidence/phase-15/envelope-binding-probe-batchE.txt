0. WHAT IS BEING PROBED, AND WHY THIS SHAPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The criterion is conjunctive. Conjunct 2 ("a tampered post-approval edit fails
closed") was already borne by checks 1-3 of hash-link.ts and is cited by the
MERGED phase-15 record. Conjunct 1 was NOT: the journal anchor attests what
INTAKE COMMITTED, never what a HUMAN APPROVED.

So the probes have to answer two different questions:

  (a) is the NEW bearer load-bearing?          -> P1, P2(a), P3, P4
  (b) is the OLD bearer still intact, with its
      reasons unchanged?                       -> P2(b), and the restore runs

(b) matters as much as (a). A "fix" that re-labelled the existing merged
evidence would silently invalidate citations in a merged record. P2(b) is the
measurement that settles it, and it is the reason checks 4-5 are ordered last.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CLAIM: "a caller cannot declare the binding — misdeclaration is unrepresentable."
A test claiming a compile-time guarantee must be falsified, not asserted.

HALF ONE — the claim HOLDS for an object literal. A scratch file declaring

TSC_EXIT=2

HALF TWO — THE CAVEAT, AND IT IS A REAL ONE. TypeScript's excess-property check
binds object LITERALS only. A widened intermediate object, assigned through the
same field type:

So "unrepresentable" is true of the SHAPE A CALLER WRITES, not of every value
that can reach the field. Stating only half one would have been an unverified
claim wearing a measurement's clothes. This is exactly why the runtime
enforcement P1 probes has to exist, and why the ledger entry says the guarantee
is "unrepresentable at intake and unenforceable at the gate" rather than
"unrepresentable everywhere".
