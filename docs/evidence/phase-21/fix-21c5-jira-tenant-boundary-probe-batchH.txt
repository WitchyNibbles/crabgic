SECTION 5 — WHAT THIS TRANSCRIPT DOES *NOT* PROVE
================================================================================

1. It does not prove the enforcement is a tenant boundary in the full sense.
   `refuseOutOfAllowlistTenant` binds the tenant a plan DECLARES, on the
   mutation path. Reads are not tenant-checked and the remote's actual tenant
   identity is not verified. The contract's own wording
   (`packages/contracts/src/contracts/external-connection.ts:122`) says the
   field "is not a guarantee that cross-tenant access is refused", and no
   string emitted by the fixture or the gate says otherwise — asserted in both
   the pass and the fail worlds by S6/S7 and T7.

2. It does not make the enforcement Jira-side. The check is gateway-owned and
   provider-agnostic. What phase 18 now owns is the FIXTURE.

3. It does not prove the fixture's journal counter reflects the DISK. The
   fixture uses an in-memory recording stand-in, so its `journalAppends === 0`
   claim is "zero `appendEntry` calls", observed at the call boundary. The
   on-disk half is pinned by `@crabgic/gateway`'s own
   `mutation-pipeline.test.ts` ("writes NOTHING to the journal for a refusal —
   zero appendEntry calls, zero records for the key"), which probe A2 shows is
   also load-bearing.

4. In-process tests cannot prove the DEFAULT arguments (the scenario's
   `executor`, the gate's `scenarios`/`makeScenario`) stay wired to the real
   dependencies. A rewire to a behaviourally faithful frozen copy survives every
   test in both suites. PROBE A is the coupling proof for that residual, and it
   is re-runnable at any tree from the mutation stated verbatim in section 2.

5. Nothing here was run against a live or licensed system. No `@live` test was
   run, `CRABGIC_LIVE` was never set, and no workflow was dispatched.
